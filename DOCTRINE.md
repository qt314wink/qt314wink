# Design Doctrine
**Jennipher Troup · Architectures of Coherence & Meaning**

---

## About My Work

I design systems that reveal complexity without overwhelming — tools, narratives, and objects built to hold a person at the threshold of understanding long enough for something to shift.

The work looks different depending on where you encounter it. It might be a generative art suite with parametric color families and a digital-to-physical plotter pipeline. A 7-module behavioral transformation curriculum integrating CBT, Gene Keys, Jungian shadow work, and Stoic philosophy. A full-stack TypeScript monorepo with OpenAPI codegen and a canvas physics engine. A CBT journaling packet that uses narrative reframing as its primary therapeutic mechanism. A prompt chain that spirals deeper with each answer. A material-based design system that translates the refraction of light through crystal into browser-native UI rules.

What connects all of it is a single design doctrine — sometimes developed consciously, sometimes discovered in retrospect — across every medium I've worked in.

---

## The Four Principles

### Constraints Are Structural

I don’t treat limitation as a problem to route around. I treat it as the parameter that produces integrity. Token limits force precision. Sprint deadlines prevent scope creep. A single data source demands depth. A browser-native-only constraint — no frameworks, no external images — produces a generative art platform where every visual can be traced to a formula. A five-turn conversation maximum teaches resolution-seeking.

Every constraint in my systems is either designed or adopted deliberately, because unconstrained systems produce noise, not meaning. When you remove the limitation, you often remove the shape.

### Failure Is Instructive

When a print fails, it triggers documentation — not a restart. When a prompt drifts, I log the drift before I correct it. When a behavioral protocol produces regression instead of growth, that’s information about what the system was trying to protect — and it gets fed back into the next iteration.

My systems are built to learn from breaking. The failure log is not separate from the design process. It is the design process.

### Joy Is Load-Bearing

Delight, surprise, and the sensation of discovery are structural requirements in every system I build. Not because I want people to feel good — though I do — but because joy is the signal that a threshold has been crossed safely. When something makes you smile before you understand it, you’re already inside it.

Joy is not decoration. It is the proof of concept.

### Every System Improves from Being Used

Prompt libraries that generate their own next generation. Fabrication recipes refined by the prints that failed. Outreach scaffolding reshaped by the relationships it creates. Behavioral transformation frameworks with adaptive feedback loops that individualize across learning style, cultural context, and literacy level. Research syntheses that teach you how to read the next dataset differently.

The loop is the point. A system that doesn’t change when used isn’t a system — it’s a static object.
