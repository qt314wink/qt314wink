# The Synthesis Mechanism
**How I work across domains**

Every project in this portfolio began as raw material — a dataset, a set of constraints, a body of evidence, a lived experience — and passed through the same process before it became something usable. The process is not a method. It is a mechanism: something that operates on its input and produces a different kind of output, reliably, regardless of domain.

The mechanism has four stages:

**1. Immersion without agenda.** I enter the material without a predetermined conclusion. With the GRACEnet agricultural data, that meant reading 143 variable descriptions before forming a single hypothesis. With the Gene Keys curriculum, that meant sitting with the 64 Shadow expressions before mapping them to clinical frameworks. The temptation to pattern-match early is the primary enemy of genuine synthesis.

**2. Finding the emotional truth.** Every body of evidence, regardless of how technical, contains an emotional truth — a human stakes claim that makes the information matter. The GRACEnet data contains the emotional truth that restoration is combinatorial: no single practice rescues soil, just as no single intervention rescues a person. The Moonwell Curriculum contains the emotional truth that constraint was once protection: the pattern isn’t the enemy, it’s the bodyguard of a wound that hasn’t been met yet.

**3. Translation to the appropriate register.** Once the emotional truth is located, the question becomes: what form does this truth need to take to reach the person who needs it? Research becomes essay becomes policy brief becomes infographic becomes daily ritual. Technical architecture becomes design token system becomes component library becomes live demo. The form is not incidental — it is chosen for the specific gap between where the audience is and where the truth needs to land.

**4. Building for the next iteration.** Every output is also a prompt for the next one. The synthesis never ends. It loops: each time a system is used, it generates new raw material, which enters the mechanism again at Stage 1.
